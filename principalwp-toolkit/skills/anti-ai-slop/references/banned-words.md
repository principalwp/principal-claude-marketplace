# The vocabulary scan

The one pass that is a literal keyword search: search the text for each item below. The word and phrase lists are near-automatic. A hit is a rewrite of that sentence. The adverbs and empty phrases are cut only when empty (the voice-preservation rule in SKILL.md); the constructions need a moment's reading. Every fix: state the specific true thing plainly, or cut.

## Often-empty adverbs

Cut only when empty.

## Often-empty phrases

Cut when they delay the point.

it's worth noting, it's important to note, at the end of the day, when it comes to, at its core, in today's fast-paced [world / landscape / environment], in the age of, in the world of, the reality is, in terms of, with regard to, in order to, going forward, in this article, let's dive in / let's dive deep, unlock [your potential / value], seamlessly [integrate / connect].

## "Tell the reader how to feel" phrases

Exist only to instruct the reader's reaction; cut them and let the fact carry its own weight.

Here's the thing, Let that sink in, Read that again, The truth is, And honestly?, Full stop., Period., Let me explain, Hot take:, Unpopular opinion:, Spoiler alert:, It's not about X, it's about Y (as a structural device).

## Banned constructions

Scan, then read.

- **"Not just X, it's Y" / "not only X, but Y."** Say what the thing is directly.
- **Self-flattering contrasts.** "Not a generic [X]," "not just another [X]," "not vibes," and any "not [the lazy version]" credibility claim. If the positive conveys it, cut the contrast.
- **Self-certified honesty.** "Here's the honest answer," "to be fully transparent," "candidly," "frankly." Announcing honesty reads like a sales move; state the thing.
- **Self-certified diligence.** "Flagged rather than silently omitted," "surfaced rather than quietly dropped," and any similar "X rather than Y" clause. Say what's there and what's missing; let the reader conclude.
- **Benefit-recap idioms.** "You leave with [X]," "you'll walk away with [X]," "you get [X] at the end." State concretely what happens. If that was already done previously, cut the line. (Open-ended variants: the writerly-tics lens in `tells.md`.)
- **Em dashes.** Never use em-dashes. Rewrite all sentences that have an em-dash. Do not simply replace it with a comma, colon, or other punctuation.

## Mechanics

- **Contractions.** Prefer them unless the register is deliberately formal. Write "we've," not "we have."
- **One idea per paragraph.**
