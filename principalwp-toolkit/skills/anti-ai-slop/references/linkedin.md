# LinkedIn and social posts

Run this in addition to `tells.md` and `banned-words.md`, not instead. It covers what is specific to short social posts, where the failure mode is a post written to attract an audience instead of because the writer had something to say.

## Content-marketing posture

The default shape is a funnel dressed up as an observation: hook, setup, thesis, credibility, call to action. The problem is posture, not topic: it reads as written to attract engagement rather than because a person was working something out. The distinction that matters is real vs. performing, not personal vs. professional.

Funnel signs: an opening built to stop the scroll rather than say something true; data placed to build credibility rather than dropped where it is relevant; the point stated as a one-sentence positioning line; a "if you're in this situation, this was written for you" qualifier; a structure that maps to a conversion funnel rather than to how a person thinks.

Fix the posture, not the content: start from genuine experience, let the idea emerge from the telling, drop data casually where it belongs, end where the thought ends. A post can name a product, a framework, even a link. Mention it the way you'd mention it to a friend; don't place it in a funnel.

## LinkedIn-specific failures

- **Thesis-cramming.** Injecting the whole framework into a post that didn't need it: prerequisites, gap, model. Mention the idea the way you'd mention it to a friend; a post listing prerequisites has become a brochure.
- **Fake origin stories.** Invented backstory ("after 20 years of watching this pattern") to lend authority. If the real reason isn't known, ask. Don't invent one.
- **Performed vulnerability.** "I'm not sure anyone will care" and "I don't have all the answers" are real only when something is actually uncertain. Hedging bolted onto a confident pitch is performance. Make it real or cut it.
- **Closing the loop and rating the work** (`tells.md` lenses 2 and 3) are the two heaviest social tells. On social: a fact followed by "so you can imagine what that does to your backlog," and passive self-congratulation ("the hard part is done").

## Banned opening templates

A match is an automatic rewrite of the opening.

- **Contrarian hook**: "Most people think [X]. They're wrong."
- **Humble-brag confession**: "I [impressive thing]. But here's what nobody tells you..."
- **Single-line shock**: "[Bold claim]." followed by a new paragraph reading "Let me explain."

Also banned: "I've been having the same conversation with...," "Here's what I've learned after X years...," "Let me tell you a story," "Something happened recently that made me think about...," and any opening positioning the writer as wise or experienced before demonstrating it. Open with the point or a specific observation; the insight should be self-evident from what's described, never announced.

## The AI-fingerprint (formatting)

- **One sentence per line, blank line between each** is the single biggest formatting tell. Write real paragraphs of varied length with irregular rhythm.
- Emoji as bullet markers, or more than one emoji in the whole post.
- Numbered lists as the post's structural spine ("5 things I learned...").
- The "hook → 3 points → conclusion → question" template.

("Here's the thing," "Let that sink in," and the rest of the radioactive phrases are in `banned-words.md`; they hit hardest here.)

## Endings

No engagement bait: "What do you think? 👇," "Agree?," "Drop a comment below," "Repost if you agree," or any reply-farming prompt. End where the thought ends; not every post needs a question.

## Pre-publish checklist

Every check must pass.

- Opening matches a banned template (contrarian, humble-brag, single-line shock)? → Rewrite the opening.
- Every sentence its own paragraph? → Restructure into real paragraphs of varied length.
- Any radioactive phrase from `banned-words.md`? → Remove.
- Ends with engagement bait? → Rewrite the ending.
- Starts from data or an industry observation instead of the writer's own experience? → Restructure so experience leads and data supports.
- Structure is hook → setup → thesis → call to action? → Funnel; restructure to thinking-out-loud flow.
- Enumerates framework points or uses positioning language? → Cut the enumeration; say it the way you'd say it to a friend.
- Invents a backstory? → Ask for the real one.
- Performed vulnerability bolted onto a confident pitch? → Make it real or remove it.
- Reads as written to attract an audience rather than because the writer had something to say? → Rewrite until it sounds like a person thinking out loud.
