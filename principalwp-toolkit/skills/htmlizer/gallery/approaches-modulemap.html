release-dispatch.mjs — approaches and module overview

release-dispatch.mjs is the ~980-line Node worker that turns a tagged storefront release candidate into a staged rollout: build the artifacts, put them in front of one canary region, widen to a quarter of traffic, then the full fleet — purging the edge cache and writing a release record on the way out. Below: the wave-gating decision it makes, then a hand-derived overview of its functions.

Three ways to decide a rollout wave is safe to widen

Every wave moves more shoppers onto a new build, and the dispatcher has to decide — without a human watching — whether to widen, hold, or roll back. This is the ADR-031 decision recorded in the file's header comment; here are the three options it weighed. Absolute thresholds on live checkout metrics

Recommended: approach 02 (B) (why)It is the only one of the three that can stop a bad wave on its own, and unlike 03 it works at the order volume the canary region actually sees. Read more →

  


  

    Why the agent picked approach 02. Approach 01 has no failure signal, so the only thing standing between a bad build and the full fleet is whoever happens to be looking — that is the NW-101 postmortem in one sentence. Approach 03 is the better idea and the wrong tool right now: the canary region books roughly forty orders in a fifteen-minute window, so a conversion comparison is measuring noise, and holding back a control cohort costs real revenue on every release. Approach 02 gives up statistical rigour to get a verdict the dispatcher can act on: three ceilings, a three-valued verdict so a quiet window holds instead of failing, and a breach that goes straight to rollbackWave. What would change the call: if the canary cohort grew past a few hundred orders per window, 03's comparison would start to beat fixed ceilings and 02 becomes the fallback rather than the gate.
