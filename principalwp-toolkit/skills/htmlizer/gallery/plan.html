Implementation plan — gift-card redemption at Northwind checkout

Eight tickets (NW-201 … NW-208) that let a shopper pay for an order with a gift card, in whole or in part, sequenced into three phases across northwind/checkout-api and northwind/storefront. Code refs are path:line against committed main (4f9a1c2); the flagged rollout and the legacy card import run on northwind-staging first. Read the steps and flag anything you object to, make the four open calls under Decisions, and say whether to apply the rest of the steps. The overall go/no-go is computed from those answers — there is no separate verdict button.

Decisions that need your call

Four open choices. Recommended defaults are pre-selected on the two that are reversible in code — change them if you disagree. The zero-remainder and refund-routing calls have no default: each one writes itself into a stored contract that other teams read, so both need your explicit nod and leaving either blank holds the submit. Pick Other… on any call to write an option the list doesn't cover.

Sequencing — three phases

Phase 1 is self-contained: nothing calls the ledger yet, so the tables and the tender arithmetic land and get tested on their own. Phase 2 is the only phase that touches both repos, and the API half must ship before the storefront half in the same release train. Phase 3 is not a cleanup tail — the reconciler in §7 has to be running and quiet for a full day before the flag in §8 widens past staff accounts. That ordering is the gate on the rollout, not a preference.

  • Phase 1 — Ledger, lookup, tender maths (§1–§3, NW-201…203): append-only ledger in src/giftcards/ledger.ts, an enumeration-resistant lookup in src/giftcards/routes.ts, and a pure tender split beside src/pricing/rules.py. No shopper-visible change.
  • Phase 2 — Checkout wiring (§4–§6, NW-204…206): holds on the checkout session in src/checkout/session.ts, the redeem-then-charge sequence in src/checkout/place.ts, and the tender UI in northwind/storefront. Depends on all of Phase 1.
  • Phase 3 — Refunds, reconciler, rollout (§7–§8, NW-207…208): split-tender refunds, the hourly reconciler that releases stranded holds, and the staged flag. NW-209 (dormancy reporting) is tracked but not in this plan.

What the legacy card export showed

41,180 active cards came out of the retiring gift-card system. Three numbers in that export changed the plan rather than confirming it. (Implementation specifics are in the steps, not here.)

  • 68% — of legacy gift-card orders were paid entirely by the card, with no second tender. The plan had been written around a card topping up a credit-card payment; the zero-remainder path is the majority case, which is why it gets a first-class code path and its own open call rather than an if at the end.
  • 12% — 4,940 cards carry a CAD balance while the checkout they would be used in is USD-only. Rejected at lookup rather than converted (see §3); they stay spendable on the CAD storefront.
  • 0.4% — 164 cards already show a negative balance in the export, from double-redeems the old system allowed. They import clamped to zero and land on a list for finance; importing a negative would trip the ledger invariant on the very first read.

Scope and non-goals

In scope. Applying up to three gift cards to a checkout, partial and full coverage, removing an applied card, refunding a split-tender order, and importing the legacy card balances. Staff issuance keeps the admin form it already has — it just writes a ledger entry now.

Deliberately not in this plan, each for a reason rather than for time:

  • Selling gift cards on the storefront. A card sale is not a taxable sale in most of the jurisdictions Northwind ships to — tax attaches when the card is spent. That is a pricing-rules change of its own, plus delivery email, and it does not block redemption.
  • A public balance-check page. It is the same enumeration surface as the lookup endpoint (§2) with none of the checkout-session rate limit to hang off.
  • Currency conversion. A rate pinned at redemption and re-pinned at refund drifts, and the drift lands on the ledger as cents nobody can reconcile.
  • Gift cards on subscription renewals. A renewal charges off-session, with no checkout session to hold a balance against. Needs its own design.
  • Dormancy and escheatment reporting (NW-209). Jurisdiction rules differ enough to be their own project, and nothing about launch depends on it.
RESOLVED
A gift card is tender, never a discount.
Because tax is computed and remitted on the full order total. Modelling a card as a line-level or order-level discount would shrink the taxable subtotal and under-remit tax on every order it touched — the single most expensive way to get this feature wrong. Tender allocation happens strictly after compute_totals returns, so total_cents is unchanged and every finance and tax export keeps reading the same field it reads today.

The plan, step by step

Each step is accepted by default. Flag a step to object precisely — a flag needs a reason, and unresolved flags hold the submit. Open choices that need a decision are not here — they are in Decisions.

Residual items — accepted, confirmed, and tracked

These needed no call. The open choices that do are in Decisions at the top.

  • Two tabs, one card. A shopper checking out twice at once takes two holds against the same card; the second gets whatever the first left. Arithmetically correct, but the second tab shows a smaller applied amount than the shopper expects. Accepted — the tender row always shows the amount actually held, never the amount requested.
  • Abandoned-cart lockup. A card stays partly held until the hold expires, so a shopper who abandons on a phone and immediately retries on a laptop sees a reduced balance for the remainder of the TTL. Accepted over a shorter TTL, which would drop cards out of slow but genuine checkouts.
  • Staff issuance keeps the existing admin form and its approval rule; the form now writes an issue entry instead of setting a balance column. ✓ Confirmed.
  • Dust cards. 1,270 imported cards hold under $1. They import and spend normally; no special casing, no write-off. ✓ Confirmed.
  • Docs sweep — docs/checkout-tenders.md, docs/pricing-order-of-operations.md, and the storefront's docs/checkout-flow.md all describe checkout as single-tender. Tracked, not gated.
